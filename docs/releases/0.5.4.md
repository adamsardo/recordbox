# Recordbox 0.5.4

Prepared 2026-11-20 (Europe/Zurich).

Loading a track for listening on Linux now reuses cached stems. Recordbox also gains optional high-precision beat grids for both steady and changing tempos.

## Deck loading and playback

- CPU-based STEMS separation on Load is now opt-in, including after an upgrade. To enable it, select both **Separate tracks when they are loaded** and **Allow automatic separation on CPU** in **Preferences → Extensions → STEMS**. You can also start separation for one track from a stem part button. Ejecting or replacing a track cancels its separation once the last deck releases it.
- In Export and Performance, the blue dial beside Preferences adjusts master volume. Drag or scroll to adjust it, or double-click to restore the default. The adjacent meters show stereo output and audio callback load. The footer shows STEMS progress.
- Linux playback animation defaults to 30 fps. In Export mode, idle audio output is released after two seconds and reopens on demand. For a library-only session, `./recordbox --disable-load` disables deck loading. With `./recordbox --disable-play`, you can load tracks and inspect their waveforms with playback and previews disabled. The switches can be combined.

## Beat grids and track analysis

- Enable **Preferences → Analysis → Track Analysis → Use high precision BeatGrid analysis** to refine beat timing and fractional BPM against repeated attacks in the audio. It works offline in **Normal** and **Dynamic** modes. The option starts off. On two generated tracks with known beat times, the 95th-percentile timing error dropped from 4.3–4.8 ms to 0.44–0.46 ms. Timing on other recordings depends on the rhythm and clarity of their attacks.
- To update an existing grid, select **BPM / Grid** and choose **Track → Analyze Track**. Automatic analysis preserves imported grids. Explicit reanalysis keeps an existing grid when decoding fails, the audio yields no grid, the track is locked, or the grid was edited during analysis. USB exports carry sub-millisecond corrections in the DAT and EXT analysis files.
- **Analysis workers** controls how much CPU a batch can use. Automatic uses three workers on a four-core machine. On the cloud Linux test machine, analyzing the four bundled demo tracks took a median of 1.80 seconds with one worker and 0.78 seconds with three. [Settings and measurement details](https://github.com/fantuz/recordbox/blob/v0.5.4/docs/PLAYBACK-PERFORMANCE.md)

## Validation and compatibility

- The Linux release-mode suite exercised 286 tests, with 37 additional tests marked as opt-in. The packaging suite exercised 107 tests. The standalone Linux build was also exercised with a clocked virtual sound device, including Load, Eject, playback and the saved High Precision setting.
- Native package checks cover startup and rendering on both Mac architectures, Windows x64 and Linux x64. Windows and Linux packages are experimental. All four packages are unsigned, and the macOS apps are unnotarized.
- The advisory baseline records two open findings for the transitive `quick-xml 0.30.0` dependency. [Dependency advisory baseline](https://github.com/fantuz/recordbox/blob/v0.5.4/.github/security/advisory-baseline.json) · [RUSTSEC-2026-0194](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2026-0194.html) · [RUSTSEC-2026-0195](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2026-0195.html)
